Policy Search III: Actor-Critic

Combine a policy (actor) with a learned value function (critic). For continuous control, deterministic policy gradient methods — DDPG, TD3 — and maximum-entropy methods — SAC — dominate.

Pattern

  • Actor πθ(s) → action (deterministic or Gaussian).
  • Critic Qφ(s,a) → estimate of return.
  • Critic trained by TD; actor trained by ∇θ Q(s, πθ(s)).

DDPG

Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient — “DQN for continuous actions”:

  • Replay buffer + target networks for actor and critic.
  • Exploration via OU / Gaussian action noise.
  • Actor maximizes Q(s, μ(s)).
∇θ J ≈ 𝔼[ ∇θ μ(s) ∇a Q(s,a)|a=μ(s) ]

TD3 — Twin Delayed DDPG

  1. Twin critics: take min of two Q targets (underestimation bias).
  2. Delayed policy updates: update actor less often than critic.
  3. Target policy smoothing: add clipped noise to target actions.
y = r + γ mini=1,2 Qφ'i(s', πθ'(s') + ε)

SAC & REDQ

Soft Actor-Critic maximizes reward and entropy: $J = \mathbb{E}[ r + \alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s)) ]$.

  • Stochastic Gaussian policy; automatic entropy temperature α.
  • Robust exploration without hand-tuned action noise.
  • REDQ: use a large critic ensemble and a random subset min for extreme sample efficiency.

Interactive: why twin critics matter

Toy landscape: the critic overestimates one region more than another. Using min(Q1,Q2) yields a less optimistic (more reliable) action choice.
