Policy Search I: Gradient-Free Optimization

Treat the policy πθ as a black box. Sample parameter perturbations, evaluate returns, and update θ using only fitness — no backprop through dynamics.

The generic loop

  1. Sample population {θi} around current θ.
  2. Roll out each policy; record return J(θi).
  3. Update θ toward high-return samples (weighted average, CEM elite set, ES gradient).
  4. Repeat.
ES gradient (OpenAI ES): ∇J ≈ (1/nσ) Σᵢ J(θ+σεᵢ) εᵢ   with εᵢ ~ 𝒩(0, I)

ERL — Evolutionary + RL hybrids

Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning typically combines a population of actors with a critic (or uses evolution to discover policies that seed RL). Ideas:

  • Evolution explores globally; RL fine-tunes locally with gradients.
  • Population maintains diversity → better exploration than a single ε schedule.
  • Parallel rollouts map well to many CPU workers.

CEM & CEM-RL

Cross-Entropy Method:

  1. Sample θ from a Gaussian (μ, Σ).
  2. Keep top-k elites (quantile).
  3. Refit μ, Σ to elites; optionally inject uniform noise for diversity.

CEM-RL (Ma et al.) alternates CEM generations with actor-critic gradient steps — e.g. half the population trained by CEM selection, half by PPO/TD3 updates.

Why gradients still win in many labs. ES needs many more episodes for high-dimensional nets, but is massively parallel and derivative-free (robust to non-differentiable envs, sparse rewards, or noisy simulators).

When to choose gradient-free

  • Simulators with contact discontinuities or non-diff physics.
  • You have huge parallel compute and moderate action dim.
  • Hybrid use: evolve a population, then distill into one actor for deployment.
